Accession 57.51.3. Bequest of Cora Timken Burnett, 1956. Gallery 240.
Link: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39325

## Concept: "Five Lives of a Bronze" (Build the world around it)
A Chola bronze was never made to stand alone in a glass case. It is an utsava murti, a
processional image: dressed in silk, garlanded, bathed in oil-lamp light and carried through
the streets on festival nights. The Met shows her stripped, silent, spotlit. This canvas gives
her back the world she lived in, across the thousand years between the mould and the museum.

1. THE MOULD (ca. 920 CE) - a sthapati in a Kaveri-delta workshop cracks the clay mould at dawn;
   the goddess comes out of the fire for the first time. (lost-wax casting, madhuchista vidhana)
2. THE PROCESSION (ca. 950 CE) - the same bronze dressed in red silk, jasmine and gold, on a
   palanquin, oil lamps, drummers, the temple street at night, the crowd's hands raised.
3. THE BURIAL (ca. 1310 CE) - priests bury her in the temple grounds by lamplight before the
   Sultanate raids reach the delta. Chola bronzes really were buried like this.
4. THE PLOUGH (ca. 1930) - a farmer's plough strikes metal in a flooded paddy field; the hand
   of the goddess breaks the mud after six hundred years underground.
5. GALLERY 240 (2026) - the Met, evening, empty gallery. The bronze in the case. In the glass
   reflection: the procession, faintly. The world she came from, still there if you look.

Why this piece: Hardik spends every morning on a practice whose whole point is to see the
form of the Goddess. A Chola sthapati saw that form a thousand years ago and poured it into
copper. The bronze at the Met is that seeing, frozen. This entry un-freezes it.

## Prompts (image-to-image with the Met bronze as reference; keep her pose, tribhanga, crown, proportions)
P1 MOULD: "A 10th-century Tamil bronze caster's workshop at dawn, a master sthapati breaking open a
cracked clay mould with a wooden mallet, the standing figure of the goddess Parvati emerging in
still-hot copper alloy exactly as in the reference bronze (same tribhanga pose, tall crown, four
lotus-hand gestures, proportions), ash and embers, charcoal furnace glow, wet clay fragments, thatched
roof, morning light through smoke, documentary realism, 35mm photograph, muted earth palette"
P2 PROCESSION: "Night festival procession in a Chola temple town, the same standing Parvati bronze
from the reference dressed in red Kanchipuram silk, jasmine garlands and gold ornaments, carried
on a wooden palanquin by bare-chested bearers, hundreds of clay oil lamps, nadaswaram players and
drummers, devotees with raised palms, temple gopuram in the background, warm lamp light and
deep blue night, cinematic photograph, Kodak Portra colours, shallow depth of field"
P3 BURIAL: "Two temple priests lowering the standing Parvati bronze from the reference into a pit
inside temple grounds at night, wrapping her in cloth, a single brass lamp, monsoon rain, wet earth,
fear and tenderness in their faces, 14th-century Tamil Nadu, painterly realism, chiaroscuro"
P4 PLOUGH: "A farmer in a flooded paddy field in Tamil Nadu, 1930s, kneeling in mud beside his ox
and wooden plough, the raised hand and crown of the buried standing Parvati bronze from the reference
breaking out of the wet earth, green paddy, overcast monsoon sky, black-and-white documentary
photograph, grain, Henri Cartier-Bresson"
P5 GALLERY: "The Metropolitan Museum of Art Gallery 240 at closing time, empty, the standing Parvati
bronze from the reference alone in a glass case under a single spotlight, and in the reflection on
the glass a faint night procession with oil lamps and garlands, quiet, architectural photograph,
wide lens, cool gallery light against warm reflected lamp light"
