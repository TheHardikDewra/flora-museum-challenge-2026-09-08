TITLE
Five Lives of a Bronze: Standing Parvati, Chola, ca. 920

DESCRIPTION (paste)
Inspired by: Standing Parvati, Chola period, Tamil Nadu, ca. first quarter 10th century. Copper alloy, H. 69.5 cm. The Met 57.51.3, Bequest of Cora Timken Burnett, 1956, Gallery 240.
Link to the original: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39325
FLORA canvas: https://app.flora.ai/projects/ns75pfrkdk6fe2bk0kg78aynvh8e1e0t

The piece: a Chola bronze was never made to stand alone in a glass case. She is an utsava murti, a processional image, cast to be dressed in silk, garlanded, bathed in oil-lamp light and carried through the streets on festival nights. The Met shows her stripped, silent, spotlit. So instead of remixing her, I built the world around her: five true moments from the thousand years between the mould and the museum.

I. The Mould (ca. 920): the sthapati cracks the clay and she comes out of the fire.
II. The Procession (ca. 950): silk, jasmine, gold, a thousand lamps, the street.
III. The Burial (ca. 1310): priests hide her from the raids, as many Chola bronzes really were.
IV. The Plough (1932): a farmer's plough strikes metal in a paddy field.
V. Gallery 240 (2026): the case, the spotlight, and in the glass, faintly, the procession.

Process: everything on the FLORA canvas starts from the Met image as the reference node. Each life is an image-to-image generation wired to that source (Nano Banana 2), each in the medium of its own era: 35mm film, cinematic colour, oil painting, 1930s black and white, museum photography. Gallery 240 took two passes; the first lost the reflection idea, the second put her old life back on the glass. The five panels then close into one museum-label strip.

Why this piece: I spend every morning on a practice whose whole point is to see the form of the Goddess. A Chola sthapati saw that form a thousand years ago and poured it into copper. This entry is that seeing, un-frozen.
